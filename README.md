# Tsunami evacuation planning prototype

An early, local web prototype for comparing evacuation scenarios. It is designed so the model and interface can be started with synthetic example data, then updated as verified Barangay Gubat data becomes available.

See [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md) for the agreed study extent, pending data decisions, implementation stages, and next actions.

## Current geographic-data milestone

The project includes a preliminary Daet boundary and OSM road/path clip: `data/daet_barangays.gpkg` (25 barangays), `data/daet_boundary.gpkg` (one dissolved outline), and `data/daet_roads.gpkg` (1,102 features). A broader screening extent is also available in `data/daet_basud_barangays.gpkg` (25 Daet + 29 Basud barangays), `data/daet_basud_boundary.gpkg` (one dissolved outline), and `data/daet_basud_roads.gpkg` (1,551 features). The broader clip includes every mapped road/path within those municipal boundaries; it is not a selected or validated evacuation corridor and does not imply that a Basud shelter has been confirmed. Attribute coverage and source limitations are documented in [`data/daet_roads_audit.md`](data/daet_roads_audit.md); a preliminary vertex connectivity screen is in [`data/daet_basud_connectivity_audit.md`](data/daet_basud_connectivity_audit.md). Its 136 preliminary components have a QGIS navigation index at `data/daet_basud_component_review.csv`; its review fields remain unfilled. The overview map is `output/daet_basud_road_review.png` and [QGIS review steps](data/daet_basud_review_instructions.md) explain how to inspect it without treating the components as approved routes.

These GeoPackages are not yet connected to the app's evacuation calculations. The QGIS export `gubat_tsunami_exposure_map_preview.png` with PDF `gubat_tsunami_exposure_map.pdf` remains a printable/downloadable reference. The interactive GIS map in `app.py` reads the Gubat hazard/boundary GeoPackages and the Daet–Basud candidate road graph, with selectable extents and layer visibility; the user confirmed that the map renders and those controls work. It currently displays geographic layers but does not map the sample R1-R12 calculation results, whose node coordinates are not real Gubat locations.

A preliminary vertex-based graph has now been exported from `data/daet_basud_roads.gpkg` to `data/daet_basud_network_nodes.csv` and `data/daet_basud_network_edges.csv` using `data/build_osm_road_graph.py`. It preserves OSM names/refs where present and creates walking links in both directions. This is a candidate graph for review, not a validated connected route network: its geometry check reports 136 components, and road crossings, pedestrian access, barriers, grade separation, and local continuity still need review. The graph has not replaced the app's sample R1-R12 calculations. The client says there is no definite official evacuation location; schools, SM, or other client-suggested places may be assessed as **candidate receiving areas** for scenario comparisons, but permission/use, access, and usable capacity must be recorded as confirmed, provisional, or unknown. They must not be described as official shelters or verified safe sites without evidence. Geographically referenced origins and candidate coordinates are needed before geographic routing; any assumed capacities must be clearly labeled.

The app also includes a separate Daet–Basud road-coverage review panel using `output/daet_basud_road_review.png` and its PDF download. That map is for visual screening only; it is not the routing network used in the calculations. The app continues to use synthetic scenario inputs until routing, georeferenced population origins, candidate receiving-area locations and access, capacity evidence or labeled assumptions, and local review work are completed.

The Gubat origin anchors also have a reproducible proximity/component screen generated by `data/audit_origin_access.py` and saved as `data/origin_access_review.csv`. It reports anchor-to-road-line distance separately from anchor-to-candidate-node distance, OSM feature tags, and raw graph components. The current screen places Roseville 163.0 m from its nearest mapped road geometry and candidate node; Cleofe Ecology Village is 13.4 m from a mapped line (38.3 m from its selected graph node); Mandulongan is 24.6 m from a mapped line (25.1 m from its selected node). Roseville's node is in component C046 (21 vertices), while Cleofe and Mandulongan are in C005 (622 vertices). Every row remains `REVIEW_REQUIRED`: these straight-line measurements do not confirm an entrance, usable pedestrian connector, crossing, or route. The app's Road coverage panel shows and offers this report for download.

## Important scope note

The included network and population are **synthetic demonstration data**, not measurements or official recommendations for Barangay Gubat. The prototype models movement over a sample road graph; it does not simulate tsunami generation, wave propagation, inundation, warning issuance, or real-time navigation. Do not use its sample outputs for emergency decisions. Replace the example data and review assumptions with the Barangay and Daet MDRRMO before interpreting results.

## Client-provided GIS and planning inputs

- `tsunamishp/tsunami.*` is a polygon shapefile bundle with six inundation-depth classes and a WGS 84 / EPSG:4326 coordinate reference system. The client defines inundation depth as the vertical distance from ground surface to the top of accumulated water. Treat this as a static hazard extent/depth layer, not a time series or wave-motion simulation. Overlay it with verified roads, house points, and candidate destinations; exclude mapped inundated segments/sites only according to the scenario and an authorized hazard interpretation.
- The client clarified that “length of the tsunami” means its **inundation distance/extent**. That layer supports hazard-zone and route screening. It does not provide wave-arrival time or hazard duration; do not use it to claim a timed “beat the wave” result.
- Population privacy: do not collect or model person counts at individual household/building points. The project-provided `2024 CBMS Total Population.xlsx` contains a Gubat barangay aggregate (6,502 household members and 1,680 responding households); it is recorded in `data/origin_zones.csv` for planning-level use only. Confirm the table's reference period, definitions, and permitted use with the provider. If a finer origin breakdown is later approved, use safely aggregated zones (for example, sufficiently large purok/grid areas) and suppress small cells; never back-allocate totals to identifiable houses. A barangay total alone cannot identify each resident's starting point, so use one clearly labeled representative zone/access point until a vetted aggregate is available.
- OpenStreetMap can provide a starting pedestrian and road network. Verify connectivity, pedestrian access, road width, sidewalks, gates, and closures with field observation and local officials; OSM coverage is community mapped and should not be presented as exact without validation. Attribute “© OpenStreetMap contributors” and follow the ODbL terms when using its data.

The client recommends evaluating evacuation movement beyond Barangay Gubat, toward southern Daet and potentially Basud. The exposure map may remain focused on Gubat, while the evacuation-network analysis should cover the connected road corridors and candidate receiving areas. The client has clarified there is no definite official evacuation location; schools or SM are examples of places that could be considered during or after a disaster. Treat specific sites as scenario candidates only, and show their intended use, access, safety basis, and capacity as unknown unless a responsible source confirms them. A Camarines Norte OpenStreetMap roads GeoPackage is available at `data/raw/osm_camarines_norte_roads.gpkg` (5,276 line features, EPSG:4326; dataset metadata timestamp 2026-10-16). ## Provisional client assumptions to review

These are scenario inputs pending confirmation, not validated local measurements:

- Household size: test a 5-10 person range using MPDO/PSA aggregate distributions when available. Do not infer exact household sizes from the barangay total.
- Vehicle access: the one-vehicle-per-family statement is an assumption to check. Ask for aggregate vehicle ownership/availability, driver availability, vehicle seats, and pickup/boarding arrangements before modeling vehicle evacuation.
- Shelter space: the Sphere Handbook 2018 shelter guidance states a minimum 3.5 m² living space per person in the relevant warm-climate guidance, excluding cooking, bathing, and sanitation areas. The client's 2.5 m²/person assumption is different and must not be labelled the Sphere minimum. Keep both as clearly named scenarios only if advisers and responsible local authorities approve the comparison. At 3.5 m²/person, 50 people require 175 m² of usable covered living area; at 2.5 m²/person, 50 require 125 m². Actual safe capacity must also account for usable area, facility condition, access/egress, and applicable local requirements. A blanket “50 people per room” assumption needs room-by-room confirmation.

The separate route model still uses its schematic demonstration graph for capacity and group calculations. In the geographic scenario, users can select several named Gubat origin areas and enter assumed evacuee amounts under a scenario-wide cap (defaulting to the provisional Gubat aggregate of 6,502, but configurable for broader study assumptions). Anonymous groups and departure times are generated separately within each selected area's configured amount; these are scenario inputs, not measured local counts. A user can also add a session-only named origin by selecting **Map point type → Origin area**, turning on **Pick a road point**, clicking a teal road node, and naming that aggregate in the left sidebar. This adds one representative graph-node anchor, not an area polygon, neighborhood boundary, household location, or verified entrance. After adding multiple candidate receiving pins, use **Send each origin to pins** in the left sidebar to set the planned percentage from each origin to each pin (up to 100% per origin). For example, configure 80% of one origin to Pin 2 and 20% to Pin 3. In **Explore details → Pin route**, enter a maximum headcount for each pin. Whole synthetic groups are assigned in departure order toward the configured shares while respecting pin headcount limits; rounding, network gaps, and exhausted capacity can leave groups unassigned. The route table shows configured versus actual origin shares and assigned people. Pin limits are total scenario headcounts, not arrival rates or verified facility capacity. If modeling subareas such as parts of Cleofe, add separately named aggregate origins and enter the intended people amounts, then exclude any overlapping parent area from the same run to avoid double counting. The app does not infer parent/subarea relationships or split an official population total automatically. Custom origins and pins select existing graph nodes, not arbitrary off-road coordinates; graph snap offset is zero. Route details estimate path length intersecting each static maximum-inundation depth class by sampling the path at roughly 5 m intervals. This is a geometric screening comparison only; it does not model water arrival, depth at a particular time, route closures, or safety. Routes use unreviewed OSM geometry and do not model destination service rates, road queues, or fair/optimal load balancing. If an origin cannot connect to a selected pin, the app reports routing-component information and the closest disconnected node pair as a GIS review lead; it does not add a straight-line connector. Origin anchors are candidate place references, not confirmed entrances or safe pedestrian links. The graph component screen does not account for barriers, crossings, missing OSM links, or field access. No approved population totals or mapped aggregate boundaries for Del Rosario, Borabod, roadside homes, or wider Daet–Basud origins are currently connected. Do not attach resident counts to identifiable household points. Confirm locality anchors and use approved area aggregates before interpreting route or capacity outputs.

## Start locally

Requires Python 3.10 or newer.

```powershell
python -m venv .venv
.\.venv\Scripts\Activate.ps1
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
streamlit run app.py
```

The app opens in a browser on the same computer. The sample scenario is ready to explore. You can edit the CSV files in `data/` and reload the page.

## Data files

Two large GIS inputs are intentionally kept outside version control to stay within GitHub's per-file size limit: `Geottaged Population of Gubat_modified.tif` in the project root and the `data/raw/elum2025_cmn_r05.*` shapefile bundle. They remain on the original workstation but must be obtained separately and placed at those paths when setting up a fresh clone. The rest of the project files do not include those datasets.

- `data/nodes.csv`: graph nodes with a stable `node_id`, display name, and local `x`,`y` coordinates. Coordinates are schematic in the sample. For real GIS data use longitude/latitude and choose one consistent coordinate reference system; the present graph display only uses relative positions.
- `data/roads.csv`: directed road/path segments (`from_node`, `to_node`) with length in meters, width in meters, and pedestrian capacity in people per minute. Add a reverse row if movement is allowed both ways.
- `data/daet_basud_network_nodes.csv` and `data/daet_basud_network_edges.csv`: preliminary vertex graph generated from the Daet–Basud OSM GeoPackage. Edge records retain OSM way IDs, name/ref (when available), road class, access tags, and length; walking edges are represented in both directions. This candidate graph has not passed connectivity, pedestrian-access, or local-route validation and is not yet used for scenario calculations.
- `data/origins.csv`: currently synthetic origin node, estimated population, group label, and free-flow walking speed in meters per second. It still drives the schematic demo calculations. `data/origin_zones.csv` records the provisional Gubat barangay aggregate used as the upper bound for the app's scenario-wide evacuee cap. Use aggregate zones with representative access nodes only, and never include person-level counts tied to household/building locations.
- `data/origin_zone_candidates.csv`: public named-place anchors for candidate Gubat residential origins, with Del Rosario marked out of scope for the Gubat total. The app's area caps are user-entered assumptions, not observed area counts.
- `scenario_generation.py`: creates reproducible anonymous group-size and departure-time draws. The app creates groups separately for each selected origin's user-entered amount, attaches them to that origin's candidate road-graph node, and assigns whole groups toward user-configured origin-to-pin shares subject to connectivity and entered pin headcount limits. Ties among eligible pins favor the faster free-flow path. It does not model destination service rates, road queues, or balanced/fair allocation. Anchors are not household locations, official boundaries, observed population density, or validated access points.
- `data/shelters.csv`: destination node and estimated capacity in people.
- `data/scenario.csv`: scenario label and departure delay in minutes. Additional scenario controls are available in the app.

Every ID in roads, origins, and shelters must match an ID in `nodes.csv`. Values must be nonnegative; road length, width, capacity, speed, and shelter capacity must be positive. Keep a record of each data source, geographic coverage, resolution, confidence, license, and usage restrictions in `data/data_sources.csv`.

## Current model behavior

The prototype routes each origin group to its nearest reachable shelter by free-flow walking time, then estimates link queues using a simplified capacity model. It reports estimated completion time, arrival counts, unserved people, shelter overflow, and heavily loaded links. It does not represent individual people, children accompanying adults, road-crossing conflicts, vehicle boarding, panic, water depth, or route closures caused by inundation. These can be added only when data and scope justify them.
